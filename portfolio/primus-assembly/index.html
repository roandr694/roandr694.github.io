Building PRIMUS

Thirty-four steps from loose parts to a standing robot, starting inside the actuators. Each step plays as a 3D animation built from the CAD: parts come in from where you would bring them, screws turn in under a hex key and inserts go in under the iron.

This guide builds PRIMUS from loose parts: first the six actuators, then the hip modules, the body, the legs and the covers. It follows the main Fusion 360 assembly part by part, in an order the parts allow: sub-assemblies are built on the bench and then joined, screws go in before the parts that would cover them, and every screw, insert and nut in the CAD has its place in a step.

The 3D view beside the steps plays each step as an animation built from the CAD itself. It follows the step you are reading; select any action in a step to replay it, drag to look around, and use the buttons to pause, scrub or move between steps.

Before you start

Print the parts. The printed parts are listed in the bill of materials. The CAD assigns PETG to most of them, ABS to the arm cores, a carbon-filled PA12 to the landing-gear struts and stops, and TPU to the feet and plugs.

Heat-set inserts. Brass inserts go into the printed parts with a soldering iron. Each step presses the inserts its parts need just before they are used. Press them in square to the surface with the iron at the temperature the insert maker recommends, and let the part cool before loading the thread.

The actuators. PRIMUS uses actuators derived from Aaed Musa's OpenQDD V1, a 3D-printed quasi-direct-drive actuator with a 9:1 helical planetary gearbox. PRIMUS keeps the 9:1 ratio but uses a stronger, modified printed gearbox, a 130 kV motor and mjbots moteus-r4 drivers at 24 V. Chapter 1 builds one actuator part by part from the PRIMUS CAD.

Tools. 1.3 mm hex key, 1.5 mm hex key, 2 mm hex key, 2.5 mm hex key, 3 mm hex key, 4 mm hex key, Phillips screwdriver, soldering iron with an insert tip. Glue for the magnets.

Not covered yet. Wiring, cable routing and screw torques are not in the CAD, so this guide does not cover them. Where the CAD leaves something open, such as how the aluminium profiles join each other, the step says so.

1Actuators

All six joints use the same quasi-direct-drive actuator, a modified OpenQDD with a 9:1 helical planetary gearbox and a moteus driver on the back. Build the four hip actuators and the two wheel actuators the same way up to the last step; only the way they are closed differs.

1.1

Heat-set inserts in the motor casing

Start with the printed motor casing lying on its front face, back side up. The two inserts on the back hold the driver bracket later.

  1. Place the motor casing on the bench, back side up.
  2. Press the two M3 heat-set inserts into the back of the casing.
Parts in this step
  • Motor casing×1
  • M3×6 heat-set insert×2

Tools soldering iron with an insert tip

1.2

Stator into the casing

Turn the casing over. The stator drops into the casing from the front, then four M4 screws through the back of the casing clamp it.

  1. Turn the casing over.
  2. Lower the stator into the casing from the front, with its leads towards the cable slot.
  3. Turn the casing over, holding the stator in place.
  4. Fix the stator with four M4×10 socket-head screws from the back.
Parts in this step

1.3

Sun gear on the rotor

Build the rotor next to the casing. The 8-tooth helical sun gear bolts to the front face of the rotor with four M3×18 screws.

  1. Turn the casing over.
  2. Set the rotor on the bench, magnets down, shaft up.
  3. Fit the sun gear onto the rotor face, lining up the four holes.
  4. Fasten the sun gear with four M3×18 socket-head screws.

1.4

Rotor into the stator

  1. Lower the rotor straight down over the stator until the shaft seats in the bearing.

    3Frame and body

    The body is a frame of 20×20 aluminium profile. The hip modules bolt to its sides, the electronics sit inside on printed trays, and the belly with the retractable landing gear hangs underneath.

    3.1

    Frame

    Cut the profiles to length and lay out the two side ladders, the front rails, the middle rail and the two long rear rails. The corner joints between profiles are not modelled in the CAD yet; join them with 2020 corner brackets or tapped ends, and keep the channel positions used by the T-nuts in the later steps free.

    1. Lay out the X− side ladder: two 140 mm rails and two 53 mm posts.
    2. Lay out the X+ side ladder.
    3. Join the ladders with the two 228 mm front rails.
    4. Add the 188 mm middle rail on top.
    5. Add the two 400 mm rear rails.
    Parts in this step

    Shin and wheel actuator

    The shin has the same plate-and-core construction. The wheel actuator sits across the bottom end on two of the M3 standoffs.

    1. Lay the inner shin plate on the bench.
    2. Place the printed core.
    3. Press the two 608 bearings into the top end.
    4. Fit the wheel actuator, output facing out.
    5. Stand the brass standoffs, two of them through the actuator ears.
    6. Lay the outer plate on top.
    7. Screw the outer plate down.
    8. Turn the shin over.
    9. Screw the inner plate down.
    10. Turn it back, actuator output up.

    4.5

    Wheel

    The rim bolts to the actuator output flange. The axle runs through two 6001 bearings, one in the rim and one in the outer hub, so the rear link can turn freely about the wheel axis.

    1. Set the rim on the bench.
    2. Press six M3 inserts into the rim.
    3. Press a 6001 bearing into the rim hub.
    4. Stretch the tyre over the rim.
    5. Drop the inner axle collar into the output flange.
    6. Set the wheel on the output flange.
    7. Lay the cover disc on the rim.
    8. Fix it with six M3×8 countersunk screws.
    9. Press the second bearing into the outer hub and set hub and shim on the disc.
    10. Clamp hub, disc and rim to the output flange with six M3×30 low-head screws.
    11. Slide the axle through both bearings into the inner collar.
